# Artist genres

Part of [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md).


Genres of the acts you saw, so the Stats and Ranking views can show what kind of music you go out for. Code: `src/server/events/genres.ts` (shapes in `genreTypes.ts`), routes in `genreRoutes.ts`, the browser side in `src/services/genres.ts`, `src/components/ArtistGenresPanel.tsx` ("Genres" in the Table view), and `src/components/ManageEntry.tsx` (genre editing for artists in Manage). Nothing is guessed and no model is involved; an act neither source knows has no genre, and the views count it apart.

1. **music-story file.** music-story (a separate repo: your 19.5 years of Spotify and Last.fm listening in DuckDB, on your Mac) writes `build/artist_genres.json` with `uv run python -m pipeline.artist_genre export`: every artist it knows with a genre (name, MusicBrainz ID, styles with weights summing to 1, which tag sources fed it: Last.fm, Spotify, MusicBrainz) and its curated genre map (tag to style and one of 13 parent genres). You upload it in the panel (`POST /api/artist-genres/music-story`). Each act you saw is matched by its stored MusicBrainz ID (`canonical_artists.mbid`), then by name (`normalizeName`, with your renames from `artist_aliases`). Only matched acts are stored, as `artist_genres` rows with source `music-story`; uploading again replaces them. The genre map is kept in `app_settings` (`genre_map`).
2. **MusicBrainz, on request.** For acts the file does not cover, "Look up on MusicBrainz" sends 5 at a time (`POST /api/artist-genres/musicbrainz`). Each uses the stored MusicBrainz ID or the one artist whose name or alias is exactly the act's name (`exactMatches`, the rule `resolveArtist` uses); two or more, or none, means no genre. The artist's genres (`/ws/2/artist/<id>?inc=genres`, with vote counts) are read through the genre map: unmapped tags are ignored, styles under 15% of the votes dropped and the rest rescaled (`mapTags`, the same rule as music-story). The row keeps the artist page as `source_url`. A lookup that finds nothing is stored too (empty `genres`), so it is not repeated unless you choose to retry the MusicBrainz scan or re-scan in the panel.

3. **You.** In the event panel, under "Who you saw", the Genres list (`src/components/SeenGenres.tsx`) and on an artist's record in Manage (`src/components/ManageEntry.tsx`), each act shows its genres with an "Add genre" field that offers every genre you already have as you type (`knownGenres`: the genre map's styles and those stored on acts, yours included); anything else is added as a new genre, which is its own parent genre. Adding or removing a genre makes the act's genres yours (`PUT /api/artist-genres/:key`, `setOwnGenres`): the shown ones are copied and changed, stored as source `you` with equal weights. A known genre keeps its spelling and parent. "Use music-story's" (or MusicBrainz's) drops yours so the found ones show again.

Which to show (`genresByArtist`): yours when you set any (an empty list of yours means you say none), else music-story's genres when it has any, else MusicBrainz's. Stats ("Genres you see live", `genreMix`): every act you saw at every event you went to counts once, split across its genres by weight; the share is of the sets with a genre, and the rest are counted apart. Artists: each card names up to three styles (the tooltip gives the weights and the source), and a genre filter lists the parent genres present.
