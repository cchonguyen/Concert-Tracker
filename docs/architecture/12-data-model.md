Records (0014, `src/server/entities/`, contracts in `.spec/SYSTEM_SPEC.md` section 3) are dual-written for now: an event's names stay the source of truth, and `withEntityLinks` (wrapped around the store by `createStore`) points them at records on every save, creating a record for a name not seen before. A name matches a live record of the same kind by its normalized name or an alias (artists also through the pipeline's `artist_aliases`); a place is the event's city with its state and country. New records get an ID derived from kind and name, so racing saves and a re-run agree. Look-alike names (a leading "the", spacing, a place without its state) are never joined on their own: `possibleDuplicates` lists them for you. Events saved before 0014 are linked once by `backfillEntities` (the Worker's minute cron, or local startup), which also copies learned artist spellings into `entity_aliases`. Without the 0014 tables events save with their names only, as before. Both stores enforce the same rules (`entities/integrity.ts`).

A venue that is in a place (`entity_links` role `in`) gives every event there its city, state and country (`src/server/entities/venuePlace.ts`), applied by the linking store on every save and marked `sources.city/region/country = { sourceType: 'venue' }` (shown as "from its venue"). Whatever location the save brought (typed, Stage 1 research, an import) is replaced; when it was another place it is kept on the venue as an offer (`attrs.placeOffers`) for Data health, so nothing is lost and research never overrides the venue. When the venue's place only names a city (or city and state) and a save brings a missing state or country for that same city without conflicting, those added fields stay on the event instead of being cleared. Stored weather for an event whose city changes this way is kept and flagged `cityChanged`, not fetched again. A canonical edition's weather record (planned, `docs/archive/canonical-weather.md`) is a shared field like its date and venue: it holds only place, coordinates, days, URL and checked-at, never member IDs or attendance. A venue first saved with a city is put in that city. Venues from before this are handled once, after the first backfill: `backfillVenuePlaces` (Worker cron or local startup, setting `venue_places_backfill`) puts in a place only the venues whose events all use one city, filling their events without a city, as one undoable change; venues whose events disagree keep no place, and `GET /api/entities/health` lists them with the cities their events use. Setting a venue's place in Manage rewrites its events the same way (undoable); merging two venues keeps the target's place.

Manage operations (`src/server/entities/manage.ts`, `/api/entities`) change records: rename, merge, add or remove an AKA, link a venue to its place or a series to the organization hosting it, and add an organization by hand (every other record comes from your events' names). A rename or merge also rewrites the name on every event that points at the record, whichever of its names the event used, so text and records never disagree; a rewritten venue, city, state or country is marked as yours (`sources`). The old name becomes an AKA, so typing it again finds the record. A name or AKA another record already has is refused with that record, to merge into instead. Merge points the source (and anything merged into it) at the target, moves its AKAs and links (dropping duplicates), and on an event that had both, keeps one act or person: the target's marks first, the source's filling what it lacks, B2B partners following the new key. Places have no AKA; they merge. Every change writes its manifest to `entity_history` first, then records, AKAs, links and events; undo puts back the records and every event not changed since, and is refused while a later change touched the same records. A renamed artist's acts get the new key, so a canonical lineup still listing the old spelling no longer marks them as seen in lineup review.

Catalog artists as records (GA3, `src/server/entities/canonicalManage.ts`, owner only: `PATCH /api/manage/canonical/artists/:key` rename, `POST .../:key/merge` with `into`, `POST` and `DELETE .../:key/aliases` for an AKA) change only `canonical_artists`, `artist_aliases` and `performances` (the edition links). They never write a member's `logged_events` or `seen` text. A rename gives the artist its new key (its performances move with it), keeps the old name as an AKA, and repoints AKAs that named the old name to the new one. A merge points the source at the target: the target keeps its MBID, or takes the source's when it has none; the source's name and AKAs become AKAs of the target; each performance moves, and on an edition where both had one, the target's row keeps its fields, takes the source's billing when it has none, and keeps both sources. An AKA is stored normalized with the artist's name it points to, the same table the lineup pipeline reads, so a name or AKA another artist holds is refused with that artist (merge them instead). Because lineup linking reads `artist_aliases` when it shows events, a rename or AKA changes how members' events link and read; their stored text does not change. The owner's own artist record follows a rename or merge only through the existing entity operations (`renameEntity`, `mergeEntity`), which rewrite the owner's own events as they always do; the step records that record's history ID and undo runs it back first. AKA edits do not touch the personal record. Each change writes its manifest to `entity_history` first (operation `canonical-rename`, `canonical-merge`, `canonical-alias-add` or `canonical-alias-remove`, with a `canonical` field holding the before and after of the artists, performances and AKA rows), then writes in an order the foreign keys allow: MBIDs freed, artists, performances, AKAs, then the artists removed. Undo (`POST /api/entities/history/:id/undo`, which dispatches on the manifest) is owner only (403 otherwise), is refused (409) while any touched artist, performance or AKA differs from what the change left, and marks the entry undone. Not built yet: a merged-away artist is deleted, not kept as a pointer; the legacy `seen_sets` table, which still references `canonical_artists`, is not moved (the table is no longer written); and AKA edits do not follow to the owner's personal record.

Editions and series as records (GA4 & Global Archive Series/Weekends, `src/components/CanonicalArchive.tsx`, `src/services/reviewRows.ts`, owner only). `event_editions.series_slug` is nullable (`ON DELETE SET NULL`), so standalone concerts do not generate stub `event_series` rows. Canonical series (`event_series`) have `kind` (`'festival' | 'tour'`, default `'festival'`), optional `brand`, `aliases`, `city`, `region`, and `country`. A festival series (`kind = 'festival'`) holds its home place (`city`, `region`, `country`) at whatever granularity applies; missing place fields on attached editions resolve on read from the series (`Edition ?? Series`), while edition values always win when set. A tour series (`kind = 'tour'`) is location-agnostic (`city`, `region`, `country` null and disabled), with individual tour stops carrying their own venue, city, region, country, and dates on `event_editions`. Automatic type inheritance passes the linked series's `kind` as a read-only `seriesKind` signal into `eventScale()` (`festival` -> `'festival'`, `tour` -> `'concert'`) via the existing `event_series` join in `EDITION_HEADER_COLUMNS` / `EDITION_SELECT_WITH_SERIES` (zero extra DB queries); `logged_events.kind` strictly stores only the user's explicit choice, with precedence `User choice (e.kind) > Series kind (e.seriesKind) > Day/Act/Clashfinder rules`. Forms for `ArchiveSeries`, `SeriesDetail`, and `EditionFactsForm` share `SuggestInput` (`Brand`, `City` via `/api/places/cities`) and `/api/places/guess` 1-tap `"Use ... from the map"` (`placePatch`) offers; `SeriesDetail` also offers `"Use ... from attached editions"` when the series place is empty and attached editions agree. In `EditionFactsForm`, toggling `Multiple weekends` detects whether existing dates belong to W1 or W2, places them into the matching slot, and pre-fills the sibling weekend at `±7 days` as a clearly marked draft (`"Draft (-7d) — confirm"` or `+7d`), requiring an owner `Source note` before saving (never auto-saved). Canonical series support create, edit, `merge_canonical_series` (re-pointing editions and absorbing aliases), and attach/detach on editions via `/api/manage/canonical/series*`. Duplicate editions can be merged via `mergeCanonicalEditions` (`POST /api/manage/canonical/editions/:id/merge`), with an optional `"Combine dates into Weekend 1 & Weekend 2"` mode that builds `event_editions.weekends` (`EditionWeekend[]`) and preserves `logged_events.weekend` on linked events. Edition facts (name, start and end dates, venue, city, region, weekends) are edited with "Edit facts" on the detail: the form shows each changed fact as old to new, and Save stays disabled until a fact changes and a source note is given. The route is `PATCH /api/manage/canonical/editions/:id/facts` (`editEditionFacts` and `parseEditionFacts` in `src/server/lineup/canonicalIngest.ts`). A blank fact reads "Not known" and stays null; nothing is defaulted. The note is stored on the edition in `event_editions.fact_source_note`, added by migration `0034_edition_fact_sources.sql`. The migration is checked into the repo (additive, idempotent, one nullable column). Deployment status not verified: confirm it on the live database before relying on the note. The note is shown as "Facts source" on the detail. The edition key and status do not change with a fact edit, and re-confirming keeps the note. An edition's detail (`GET /api/manage/canonical/editions/:id`, backed by the `edition_lineup_facts(p_edition_id)` SQL RPC in a single query) shows its series, brand, year, dates, weekends, venue, city, region, country and status, and each act with its billing role ("Artist" when none is stored), B2B partners, set, days, stages, set times and sources. Set times come only from a source that times them. Each source shows its link and its quote, or the "Admin curated" badge for the owner's own curation. Missing data reads "Not known" and is never filled in. "Edit lineup" reopens `LineupReview` in canonical mode, seeded from the saved edition's own acts (`reviewSubjectFromEdition`), so nothing is looked up or added. Saving first shows the merge diff: the same request is sent to `POST /api/manage/canonical/confirm` without `apply`, and the diff lists added, changed and unchanged acts, blank details filled and conflicts kept. The second press applies that same request, and any change to the acts after the preview asks for a new preview. Re-confirming merges as the confirm rules above describe: a stored act the review does not name, or unticks, stays in the archive (removing an act is not built), and a changed act keeps the sources it was stored with, with the owner's curation source added once. Delete and demote to draft are out of scope. For egress efficiency, list views and matchers use lean headers (`getEditionHeaders`, and `listEditionHeaders` in `match.ts`/`dedupeProvisional.ts`) plus `listEditionArtistRefs`, archive search is debounced >=300ms, and the canonical artists list is lazy-loaded; the edition list shows "Showing 200 of N" when a search or status filter has more matches than the first 200 (`GET /api/manage/canonical/editions` returns `total` and `hasMore`).

The Manage tab (`src/components/ManageView.tsx`, `ManageEntry.tsx`) picks its section from one menu (`MenuButton`, shared in `src/components/ui/`) on the same row as the search box, on the right: the record kinds, then Data health and History. For the owner, a `Segmented` scope switch above that row chooses My records or Global archive (see the Global archive paragraph above for the archive's sections and filters). The search box shows on a record list only. It lists the live records of each kind with how many events point at each (from the IDs saved on events), a look-alike badge from `possibleDuplicates`, and a search over names and AKAs. A record's page has Rename everywhere, its AKAs, Merge into another (typed pick, or a look-alike in one tap, always confirmed), its events, and its links: a venue's place, a series' hosts (Hosted by) and an organization's series. A name refused because another record has it offers Merge into that record. Every change shows Undo, and History lists every change with Undo. The Artists tab's cards (In Manage), the Table's Series button and the event view (Edit on every event in Manage: the event's venue, city, series and people) open the record in Manage. AKAs are stored normalized, so they show in lower case. A person's first and last name and a note are set with `PATCH /api/entities/:id/person` (`updatePerson`): the record's name becomes "First Last" and is renamed everywhere like any rename (the old name kept as an AKA), and the parts and note are kept in `attrs` (`first`, `last`, `note`); a note alone changes only the record. Both are undoable. `src/services/people.ts` works out how a person is shown: the full name, then first name and last initial ("Mike R."), then initials ("MR"), with a shorter form that would read the same as someone else's kept one step longer for both. Before you set the parts, the first word is read as the first name, for display only. `PersonName` (`src/components/PersonName.tsx`) shows one person in the space it is given: it measures the three forms and the element's real width (a ResizeObserver, since a name's length decides where it must shorten, which fixed container-query breakpoints cannot) and shows the longest that fits; the full name is the tooltip and shows on a long press. `PeopleProvider` in `App.tsx` builds the forms over everyone on your events, with the names you set on person records (fetched again when events change; without 0014, names as typed). The Table's With column is no longer shown (Companions stay in the "Went with" filter and the event view), and a person's Manage page has First name, Last name and Note fields in place of the plain rename.

**Labels and details** (Manage step 5). Each kind's details are listed in code (`ATTR_FIELDS` in `src/server/entities/attrs.ts`: a venue's address, type, capacity and note; a note for artists, series and organizations; a label's group), so a new one needs no migration; `PATCH /api/entities/:id/attrs` (`updateAttrs`) sets them in your words, an empty value removes one, other keys in `attrs` are left alone, and it is undoable (history operation `details`). Labels are records (kind `label`, migration `0016_entity_labels.sql`), added in Manage with an optional group (`POST /api/entities` with `kind: 'label'` and `group`), and put on venues and series with `has_label` links; a record has one label per group, so Outdoor replaces Indoor (one undoable change). `src/services/dataHealth.ts` works out the Data health lists from your records, links and the IDs on your events: `categoryGaps` (a group used on one venue or series becomes a field for all of that kind; these lack it), `linkedSuggestions` (a series and the venues its events were at: when one side has a group's label and the other has none in that group, the other is offered it; offers only), `rareLabels` (on one record or none, often a typo), `venuesWithoutPlace` and `placeOfferVenues` (venues where a save brought another city than the venue's place). In Manage, a Labels list adds labels with a group (past groups offered); a venue's or series' page has Labels (its labels, the series' or venues' offer as "Desert Fest is Outdoor. Add Outdoor", "Set indoor/outdoor" chips when it lacks a group others of its kind use, and a picker) and Details (the fields from `ATTR_FIELDS`); a label's page lists what it is on. Data health adds those lists with one-tap fixes: labels some records lack, offers from linked records, labels used once or not at all, other cities a save brought, and venues with no place.

**Insights** (Manage step 6, `src/services/insights.ts`), worked out from the events you went to each time and never stored. An artist's gap since you last saw them and longest wait between two shows (`artistGaps`) use only events dated to the day and say so when some are not; they show on the Ranking card and the artist's Manage page. A person's page has your shows together, the first one, and the acts you have only seen with them (`personInsights`, by the person's ID on your events), and "Introduced you to": a `introduced_me_to` link from the person to artists (no migration; roles are checked in code), shown on the artist's page as "Introduced by". A series' page lists the editions you went to and your longest and latest runs of consecutive years (`seriesStreak`). Data health (`src/components/DataHealth.tsx`, a category in Manage) lists what needs your call: venues whose events disagree on the city (from `GET /api/entities/health`), where picking one of the cities puts the venue in it and rewrites its events, a button that reruns the agree-only venue pass, and look-alike groups with a one-tap, confirmed merge. Nothing there changes on its own, and every change is undoable from History.

**Shared occurrences (slice 11.8; migrations `0041_shared_occurrences.sql` and `0042_merge_editions.sql` written, not yet applied and not used by code).** Plan: [`docs/ingest-canon-plan.md`](../ingest-canon-plan.md) section 3. Until slice 11.9 and later move the code, everything below is schema only, and 0041 is safe to apply before that code: the edition status still accepts `confirmed` (read as `verified`) and no row is converted. The conversion of `confirmed` rows to `verified` is a separate, commented-out step at the bottom of 0041 for a later slice.

- `event_editions.status` accepts `draft` (owner only, unchanged), `provisional`, `verified` and still `confirmed` (check constraint `event_editions_status_check`, rewritten; existing rows are not changed). New nullable columns `venue_id` (to `canonical_venues`), `subject_key` and `has_lineup`, and a partial unique index `event_editions_occurrence_uidx` on `(start_date, venue_id, subject_key)` when all three are set.
- New shared tables: `canonical_venues`, `venue_aliases` (unique per alias and venue; a source's venue ID is unique per source), `edition_external_ids` (unique on `(source, external_id)`), `edition_changes` (append-only: a trigger refuses deletes and any update except setting `undone_at` once; no foreign key, so a merged-away edition keeps its history) and `review_queue` (kinds `override`, `merge_suggestion`, `provisional`, `formatting`, `failed_lookup`). None holds a personal field; `tests/canonSchema.test.ts` checks this.
- Member entry (Slice 11.23): in the research queue message (never in save), any signed-in person's own event with a full date may create one `provisional` edition from its typed name, date, venue and city only (no external ids, country or personal fields; actor `member:entry`, reason `Member entry`). It is skipped when a verified edition already matched. Notice version 2 tells members.
- Member backfill (Slice 12.6, `src/server/canon/memberBackfill.ts`, run from the cron in `worker/index.ts`): once a cron run, members who accepted notice version 2 (role `member`, not blocked, with a user id) are walked through their own scoped store. For each dated event with no `editionId`, a full `YYYY-MM-DD` start date and a name, it writes a `provisional` shared edition through `upsertOccurrence` on the service store with actor `member:entry` and reason "Member backfill". Only the name, the date, an end date the person typed, and the venue and city only when the person typed them are passed on; attendance, price, notes, companions, rating and any user or event id are never read into the edition or its change record. Year-only and undated events and linked events are skipped; owner and blocked members and members on an older notice are not read. Progress is a cursor per member in that member's own settings (`canon_member_backfill`: event ids done, and `complete`), so a finished member costs one read a run; `listUserSettingsByKeys` fetches both `notice_accepted` and `canon_member_backfill` per member in one query. A few items run per cron run (default 5 across all members) inside the subrequest budget, and an item cut off by the budget is retried later. Worst case per item is 16 subrequests. Matching (`src/server/canon/match.ts`): an incoming show with the same date and subject key matches a shared edition with the same venue (by alias, or by venue text when the venue is unknown). No venue matches an edition with no venue, so one date, subject and venue is one edition. Duplicates already written (the KREAM incident, 2026-11-14) are folded by `src/server/canon/dedupeProvisional.ts`, run from the same cron block before the backfill: only `provisional` editions are grouped, the one with the most acts is kept, and the rest go through `mergeEditions` (recorded, never a verified row). At most 40 merges a run. In addition, `dedupeProvisional.ts`, `match.ts` (when collapsing ambiguous matches in `upsertOccurrence`), and `backfill.ts` (on occurrence conflict) automatically merge matching shared editions when one has 0 lineup and the other has a populated verified lineup via `mergeCanonicalEditions`. The owner can set the app setting `member_backfill_override` to `{ "until": ISO time }` to let the backfill walk members who have not accepted notice version 2 until that time (Slice 12.10); it expires by itself.
- DB performance & RLS hardening ([`supabase/migrations/0054_db_perf_rls_and_lineup_flag.sql`](../../supabase/migrations/0054_db_perf_rls_and_lineup_flag.sql), written in Slice 21.4; pending operator apply): re-affirms `PUBLIC, anon` revocations on `owner_user_id()` and `canonical_*` RPCs (retaining `authenticated` for `owner_read` and `canonicalClient()`), drops legacy open `0001` policies, rewrites all 19 `auth_rls_initplan` policies with `(select ...)` scalar subqueries, adds covering FK indexes plus `idx_logged_events_pending_research`, adds composite PKs on `venue_aliases`, `edition_external_ids`, `entity_aliases`, and `user_entity_aliases`, backfills `event_editions.has_lineup = true`, normalizes US country aliases, cleans legacy `email_imports` calendar venues, and promotes `refused_hosts` to `app_settings`.
- Lineup casing: in `src/server/lineup/pipeline.ts` (`stageMerge`), `spellings` is seeded from `canonical_artists` (`getArtists` for the job's normalized claim keys) before overlaying the member's `yourSpellings(seenNames)`, so catalog stylizations override all-caps sources while the member's own seen spelling always wins.
- Row level security is on for all five: members read the three catalog tables, only the owner reads `edition_changes` and `review_queue`, and only the service key writes.
- `logged_events.captured` (name, year, month, dates, venue, city, region, country as first typed or imported; filled from the current fields for existing rows, then a trigger keeps it from being overwritten) and `logged_events.overrides` (default empty). Reads do not resolve them yet.
- `merge_editions(keep_id, drop_id, p_actor, p_reason)` (0042, service key only) moves linked events, the legacy `attendance` rows, performances (duplicates of the kept edition's are dropped), external IDs, lookup jobs and review rows to the kept edition, records the merge in `edition_changes`, and deletes the dropped edition, all in one call.

**Store methods for shared occurrences (slice 11.9; `LineupStore`, `fileStore.ts`, `supabaseStore.ts`, `tests/canonStore.test.ts`).** Both stores behave the same; nothing calls them yet. Service key only.

- Venues: `upsertVenue` (stable id from the normalized name and place, using `normalizeName`; also records the name as an alias), `findVenueByAlias` (null when no venue or more than one holds the alias, never guessed), `addVenueAlias` (idempotent; a source's venue ID held by another venue throws `ExternalIdConflictError`).
- External IDs: `findEditionByExternalId`, `addEditionExternalId` (returns `added` or `exists`; the same pair on another edition throws `ExternalIdConflictError` and changes nothing).
- Edition changes: `recordEditionChange` (append-only), `listEditionChanges` (newest first), `markChangeUndone` (sets `undone_at` once; false the second time).
- Review queue: `addReviewItem`, `listReviewItems({ status, kind })`, `resolveReviewItem` (open items only). `assertNoPersonalFields` rejects the keys companions, notes, rating, price and attendance at any depth, in review payloads and in change before and after values (`PersonalFieldError`).
- `mergeEditions(keepId, dropId, actor, reason)`: the `merge_editions` function (0042, parameters `keep_id`, `drop_id`, `p_actor`, `p_reason`) in Supabase; in the file store the same moves in memory (events, acts without duplicates, external IDs, review rows, lookup jobs), a recorded change, and the dropped edition deleted.
- `isVerifiedStatus` (`store/types.ts`) reads `confirmed` and `verified` as the same; older call sites are not changed yet.

Store selection: Supabase when `SUPABASE_URL` is set and the migration has been applied; otherwise a JSON store under `DATA_DIR` (default `./data`). Set `LINEUP_STORE=file` to force the local store. The local store does not survive a redeploy, so use Supabase for real data. On Cloudflare the server refuses to start without Supabase.

The browser keeps nothing of yours: every view reads the server (the Map view keeps city points in `localStorage` so each city is looked up once). The theme (Dark, Light or Match system, and an accent color) is a per-device choice in `localStorage` (`src/services/theme.ts`, picked in `ThemePicker` in the header): a script in `index.html` sets `data-theme` and `data-accent` on the page before it draws, and `src/index.css` redefines Tailwind's zinc and violet color variables for them, so components keep writing `zinc` and `violet` classes. The interface is one page (`src/App.tsx`): the capture line and a summary of your log (`CaptureBar`, `EventsHome`, worked out in `src/services/home.ts`), a "See your year" tile for the current year (`YearTile`, from `yearInReview` over the shows you went to, the same count Stats shows; it opens Stats, where the share link is made, and is hidden when this year has no shown events yet), an event detail for details, seen acts, the lineup and your notes (`EventDetail`, `LineupReview`, shown by `DetailShell`), the Table view (`TableView`), and the Ranking view (`ArtistsView`, worked out in `src/services/artists.ts`: one summary per seen act, sorted and grouped by most seen, recently seen, first seen or name; the order and the Compact or Detailed view are remembered in `localStorage`; `jumpTargets` gives the letter or year jump bar). The Table view filters, sorts and summarizes the same event list in the browser (`src/services/table.ts`). Its columns are Date, Event, Where, Seen and Details: Details shows the series, Concert or Festival and formats as pills, the Went state (Upcoming, Did you go?, Didn't go), icons for a ticket email, a missing date or pick, an unreviewed lineup or a confirmed one (each opens the event, with its name on hover and for screen readers), and the rating as a star and number when rated. Details does not sort. Picking a sort key (the Sort menu, or a column header) cycles it: its usual direction (Date, Seen, Rating and Price biggest first; Event, Venue and City A to Z), then the other way, then "Order added" (newest added first, ties broken by id; `sort=none` in the address; its Sort menu choice shows "Sort by order added (newest first)" on hover). The arrow on the sorted key shows its direction. When sorted by date, a Today line sits between the future and the past (`todayLineIndex`), grouping events on today with past events. Filters sit behind one Filters button (`FilterButton`, `FilterPanel`, `FilterSelect`, `FilterToggle` in `src/components/ui/FilterControls.tsx`), the same pills the Map and the four timeline pages use. The Table keeps its filters in the address (`?view=table&...`), and holds the CSV export, built from the rows it shows (`src/services/format.ts`). Apart from "Import a list" and "Google Sheets" (see Importing a list and Google Sheets sync) it reads nothing beyond `GET /api/events` and writes nothing. "Fill gaps" (`FillGaps`, gaps worked out by `hasGap` in `table.ts`) first lists "Results to confirm": events with a year and a city, no date, and candidates waiting for a tap (`batchResultsWaiting`, Slice 3.2), each candidate one tap (`POST /api/events/:id/details/pick`) and never applied before. Then it lists the shown rows missing one thing, each with a field that saves through `PATCH /api/events/:id`; it can run stage 1 research for them one by one, queue lineup lookups (`POST /api/events/lineups`), accept a found lineup in one tap (every act not flagged, through the usual confirm), offer artists named in the titles of the rows missing who you saw (`POST /api/events/name-artists`, from stored data only; then, on request, `GET /api/events/:id/name-artists` row by row for MusicBrainz), with source-backed ones ticked and MusicBrainz ones left for your tick, saved only by "Add", pick a favourite set among the acts you saw, look up the weather for every row that has a past date and a city, and look up the state and country of each city (`GET /api/places/guess`), saved only by "Use" or "Use all". The Stats view (`StatsView`, worked out in `src/services/stats.ts`) is read-only over the same list too, and includes your series (`seriesStats`: editions, years, longest run of consecutive years, cities, acts, and acts seen at two or more editions), only for events you linked; its bars and rankings open the Table with matching filters. The Map view (`MapView`, worked out in `src/services/map.ts`, drawn with Leaflet on OpenStreetMap tiles, darkened in CSS in the dark theme) uses the Table's filters and address format (`?view=map&...`; switching between Table and Map keeps them), groups events by stored city, and places each city through `GET /api/places/point`. The four timeline pages (`TimelineTab` in `src/components/timeline/`, `?view=agenda`, `?view=month`, `?view=heatmap` or `?view=river`; each is an Explore subtab) use the Table's filters and address format like the Map, and hold an "N to confirm" chip that jumps to the first event waiting for your answer, "Open in Table" and the No date yet tray, so they stay put as you move between the views. Picking the chip from another view opens Agenda at that event. Every view takes the same props (`TimelineViewProps`: the model, a size, a range, a selected day and a selected event kept apart, and `onOpen`); only the large size is built. Agenda (`agendaBlocks`) lists events by month under each year, newest first by default (a "Newest first / Oldest first" toggle in its header, remembered in `liveArchive.agendaOrder`; the header also counts the events shown, `agendaCount`), and newest first is the same list read backwards, so the Today line still sits between the future and the past, an event running into the next month in the month it starts, year-only and month-only events first in their year or month, a Today line after today's events, and one line for each run of empty months; it opens at the Today line. Heatmap (`heatmapYear`, `heatmapRows`) draws one row per year, newest first, a square per day in Sunday-first week columns (53, or 54 when a leap year starts on a Saturday), each year as one SVG with the browser's own tooltip on hover; runs of empty years collapse into one line, this year is always shown, and tapping a lit day lists its events under that year. Month (`monthPage`, `?month=YYYY-MM`) is a Sunday-first calendar page moved by arrows, a month and year picker and Today, reported up through `onRangeChange` so the address keeps the month; a one-day event sits in its day, a run of days is a bar across them, cut at the week's edge and carried on the next row, with the days you went solid and skipped days faint, overlapping events on separate lanes, and events with only that month in a "This month, day unknown" strip. River (`riverLayout`) puts events on a left-to-right axis you drag or scroll, at four zoom levels (pixels per day, about a decade down to a month), opening on a Today line and keeping the same moment in the middle when you zoom; one-day events are dots and runs of days bars, sized in steps by acts you logged (none logged draws like one act, never "0 acts"), stacked on lanes so nothing overlaps, with names beside the marks once zoomed in. A mark opens a popover (`riverStretch`) listing up to three events that touch its days, earliest first, with date, place and attendance, and "and N more" opens Agenda at the mark's event; hover on a mouse, tap on a touch screen, Enter on a keyboard. The popover sits over an HTML hit area (`Popover` in `src/components/ui/`) because SVG marks cannot hold it; each mark keeps its `<title>` as the screen-reader name. Each view reads one model from `buildTimelineModel` in `src/services/timeline.ts`, given the filtered events and today's date. An event's span runs from its start date (else its earliest ticked day) to its end date (else its latest ticked day, else the start); the days you went are the ticked days, or every day of the span when none are ticked. Each day lists all its events and shows the strongest state (waiting to confirm, festival, show, upcoming). An event with only a year or a month is never placed on a day, one with no date goes in the No date yet tray, and events you didn't go to are left out. Dates stay `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with day maths through `Date.UTC`.

## Writing an edition (`writeEdition`)

Every edition save goes through `writeEdition` in `src/server/canon/editions.ts`; nothing else may call `store.upsertEdition` (a test greps `src` for it). `upsertEdition` syncs `has_lineup = true` whenever `performances.length > 0`. It reads the current row, saves the new one, then records one `edition_changes` row holding only the fields that changed (name, dates, venue, city, country, status, venue id, subject key, has-lineup, and a performance count as a summary), with who (`owner:<id>`, `system:ingest`, `system:learning`) and why. A write that changes nothing records nothing. There is no single Supabase call for row plus change and no migration adds one, so the row is written first and the change second; a failed change write is retried once and logged, and the row is never rolled back silently. `undoEditionChange` puts the old values back through `writeEdition` and marks the change undone. It refuses (`stale`) when the edition no longer holds what that change wrote, so a newer edit is never overwritten.
